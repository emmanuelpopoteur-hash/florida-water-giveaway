Additional perspective · EWG

Compare EWG's records by water system.

The Environmental Working Group (EWG) publishes historical utility testing data and compares some results with its health guidelines. For example, its OUC record lists 7 of 16 detected contaminants above EWG guidelines (data through 2023). Orange County Utilities has separate East, South and West records, with 3 of 11, 12 of 29 and 6 of 19 respectively (data through 2024). These counts are not the number of federal legal violations.

Read it in context

Three checks before drawing conclusions.

01

Identify the provider

Look at your water bill. In an apartment or a community with included utilities, ask the manager which public water system supplies the address.

02

Choose the right report

Providers can operate more than one service area. Match your address or system name to the report before reading its measurements.

03

Separate system data from home data

An annual report summarizes testing across a public system. Plumbing and conditions at your own tap can differ. A targeted home or laboratory test answers a different question.

Using a private well?

Your well needs its own testing.

A utility report does not apply to a private well. The EPA recommends annual testing for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, total dissolved solids and pH, with additional tests based on local conditions and concerns.
