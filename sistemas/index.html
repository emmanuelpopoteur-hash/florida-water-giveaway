A guide to your options

Different water needs.
Different solutions.

A softener, a filter, and a drinking water system do different jobs. What makes sense depends on your water source, your concerns, and the results of appropriate testing.

Three common starting points

Start with the problem you want to understand.

These are examples of technologies to discuss, not a diagnosis or a promise that a particular system will treat your water.

01

City water at home

Hard water can leave scale on fixtures. A softener addresses hardness minerals. For chlorine taste or odor, an activated carbon filter may be an option when its specific performance claims match the concern.

Consider: Your utility's water quality report and a check of conditions inside your home.

02

Private well water

Wells can have different issues, such as sediment, iron, or sulfur odors. Treatment should follow the measured issue. If you are concerned about bacteria or other health-related contaminants, arrange suitable laboratory testing.

Consider: Testing the well for the contaminants relevant to your location and situation.

03

Drinking and cooking water

An under-sink reverse osmosis system treats water at a particular tap. It is a different choice from a whole-home softener or filter. Check the exact model's certified reduction claims and maintenance requirements.

Consider: Which substances you want to reduce and the verified claims for the system you choose.

What a visit can tell you

Ask what was actually tested.

An initial home visit can help identify the water characteristics that were measured and explain possible next steps. It does not, by itself, establish whether every contaminant is present or absent.

Questions about lead, PFAS, or microorganisms need tests selected for those substances, often through a qualified laboratory. We can discuss which information would be useful for your home.
