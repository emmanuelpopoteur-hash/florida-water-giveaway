UTILITY BILLS · CENTRAL FLORIDA

Why are Toho water rates increasing?

What the utility announced, how actual bills can differ and what homeowners should check first.

Legacy Water USA · October 30, 2026 · Independent explanation of public utility information

What has Toho actually announced?

Toho states that water, wastewater and irrigation rates adjusted by 7.5% on October 1, 2025, with additional annual 7.5% adjustments each October 1 through fiscal year 2028. Toho also lists 4% annual adjustments during fiscal years 2029 and 2030. Reclaimed-water pricing is governed by a separate adjustment schedule.

Read Toho's official explanation of possible bill impacts.

Why your total bill may differ from a rate increase

A 7.5% change to published utility rates does not mean every customer's total monthly statement will rise by exactly 7.5%. Usage, wastewater charges, irrigation, other fees, billing-cycle length and prior balances can affect the amount due. Toho explains that some eight-digit accounts also carry City of St. Cloud charges such as stormwater or garbage.

Illustrative calculation—not a bill prediction

For a simple example, multiplying a $100 charge by 1.075 gives $107.50 after a 7.5% adjustment, if the entire charge were subject to that adjustment and consumption stayed constant. A real statement can be different because not every line item uses the same rate structure.

What to check if your bill jumps

  1. Compare gallons used, not just dollars, with previous billing cycles.
  2. Check the number of billing days, which Toho notes can vary.
  3. Look for irrigation issues, running toilets or hidden leaks.
  4. Review whether city fees, a previous balance or estimated meter readings affected your statement.
  5. Contact Toho for billing questions and review its conservation or irrigation assistance programs.

Will an in-home water filter lower my Toho bill?

Generally, no. A water filter or softener treats characteristics of the water used at home; it normally does not lower your municipal rate or the number of gallons you are billed for. If your goal is to reduce your utility bill, focus first on leak repair, irrigation settings and usage. If your goal is to address taste, odor, hardness or drinking-water preferences, evaluate those needs separately.

Primary source: Toho Water Authority, Possible Water Bill Impacts, accessed October 30, 2026.
